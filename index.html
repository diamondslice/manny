No-Cook / Minimal-Cook Meals

These are friction killers. Not every meal needs to be meal-prepped, macro-perfect, or chef-level. Sometimes a turkey melt + fruit is exactly the right move.

Rotisserie Chicken System

One bird from Costco (~$5) = 6-8 servings, zero cooking, already seasoned. Buy it on the way home. The carcass makes soup. Nothing goes to waste.

Rotisserie Rice Bowl
~4 min · zero cooking
▶
  • Shredded rotisserie chicken (cold is fine)
  • Rice from fridge, microwaved
  • Garlic yogurt sauce
  • Cucumber slices
  • Hot sauce or chili crisp

Strip the chicken while the rice heats. Bowl in under 5 minutes.

Rotisserie Chicken Soup
~20 min · uses the carcass
▶
  • Rotisserie carcass (after stripping the meat)
  • Water
  • 1 bone broth powder packet
  • Frozen vegetables
  • Rice or noodles
  • Shredded leftover meat
Simmer the carcass in water + broth packet for 20 min. Strain. Add frozen veg and rice, cook 5 more min. Stir in leftover chicken. This is one of the highest-satiety meals in the whole system and it costs almost nothing.
Rotisserie Quesadilla
~5 min · one pan
▶
  • Shredded rotisserie chicken
  • Cheese blend (fat-free + 2% shredded)
  • Tortilla
  • Salsa + yogurt crema for dipping

Fill, fold, press in a dry pan 2 min per side until crisp. Cut into wedges. Done.

Quick Tikka Chicken Bowl
~5 min · sauce from pantry
▶

Protein Building Blocks
Velveted Chicken Breast
~8 portions · 4 lb · fridge 3-4d · freeze ✓
▶
Primary LeanBowlsPastaWraps
Ingredients
  • 4 lb chicken breast, sliced thin against grain
  • 4 egg whites
  • 2 tsp baking soda
  • 4 tsp cornstarch
  • 2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 tsp neutral oil (optional)
Method
  1. Slice thin, keep uniform
  2. Mix egg whites, baking soda, cornstarch, salt — toss with chicken
  3. Rest exactly 15–30 min
  4. Optional: rinse lightly, pat very dry
  5. Par-cook in hot pan or blanch — don't fully cook
  6. Cool fast, portion. Finish cooking in the final dish
Use It In
Fried RiceTeriyaki BowlEnchilada WrapGarlic PastaBean Potato
Makes chicken breast feel far less like diet food. Keep lightly seasoned at prep so it fits any cuisine later. On chaotic weeks: rotisserie chicken is a direct sub for everything on this list.
Rotisserie Chicken
1 bird = 6-8 portions · fridge 4d · freeze shredded ✓
▶
Zero-Cook ProteinCostco BirdSub for Velveted
How to Break It Down
  1. Pull breast meat and shred or slice — use anywhere velveted chicken is called for
  2. Pull thigh meat separately — richer, more forgiving for reheating
  3. Strip every last bit of meat off the carcass — don't waste it
  4. Save the carcass in a bag in the freezer for bone broth if you make your own
  5. Portion shredded meat into fridge containers or freeze flat
Best Uses
  • Sub directly for velveted chicken in any bowl, wrap, or rice dish
  • Shred into soups, fried rice, and pasta — all faster than cooking from scratch
  • Cold in wraps and salads — already seasoned, needs nothing
  • Quesadillas and burritos — shred, fill, done
Costco Math
  • One bird runs about $5 and yields ~2 lb of usable meat
  • That's ~6-8 meal servings at under $1 per serving
  • Zero prep time vs. 30-40 min for velveted chicken batch
The single best friction-killer in the whole system on a chaotic week. Grab one on the way home and you have chicken for 3-4 days without turning on a stove. The skin is higher in fat — strip it if you want to stay leaner, leave it if the day allows.
Shredded Beef (Pressure Cooker)
~8-10 portions · 3-4 lb · fridge 4d · freeze ✓✓
▶
WorkhorseChuck or Top Round
Ingredients
  • 3-4 lb chuck (richer) or top round (leaner)
  • 1 onion sliced or 2 cups frozen onion
  • 6 cloves garlic or 2 tsp garlic powder
  • 1-2 cups bone broth
  • 2 tsp kosher salt, 1 tsp black pepper
  • Optional: tomato paste, cumin, oregano, chili flake
Method
  1. Season generously. Sear optional but adds depth
  2. Load cooker with onions, garlic, broth, beef
  3. Pressure cook until pull-apart tender
  4. Shred warm, return some liquid to keep moist
  5. For lower fat: chill and skim hardened fat first
  6. Freeze half if not used within 4 days

Air-Fried Potatoes & Fries
4-6 servings · best fresh · par-cooked holds well
▶
Ingredients
  • Russet or Yukon Gold potatoes
  • Oil spray
  • Salt, pepper, garlic powder, smoked paprika
Method
  1. Cut to the shape you want — wedges, fries, or cubes

Supplementation Strategy

All research-backed. Timing recommendations are based on what actually moves the needle — not marketing.

Creatine Monohydrate
5g daily · most researched supplement in existence
▶
Protocol
  • 5g daily — no loading phase needed
  • Any time of day works; consistency matters more than timing
  • Slightly better uptake taken post-workout or with a meal containing carbs, but the difference is minor
  • Mix into smoothies, cereal bowls, or post-workout shakes — tasteless and dissolves easily
What it actually does
  • Increases phosphocreatine stores → more ATP available for high-intensity effort
  • Meaningfully improves strength output, power, and rep capacity over time
  • Supports muscle protein synthesis, especially when training hard
  • Emerging evidence for cognitive benefits, particularly under sleep deprivation or stress
  • May slightly increase water retention in muscle tissue — this is normal and not fat
Creatine monohydrate is the most studied, cheapest, and most effective form. Skip the fancy variants. Increase water intake by 1-2 cups per day while using.
Magnesium Glycinate
300-400mg elemental magnesium · evening
▶
Protocol
  • 300-400mg elemental magnesium as glycinate form
  • Take 30-60 minutes before bed — the glycinate form has a mild calming effect
  • With food or without — both fine, though food reduces any chance of loose stools
  • Glycinate is preferred over oxide (poor absorption) or citrate (more laxative effect)
What it actually does
  • Majority of people are deficient — high-protein, high-exercise lifestyles deplete magnesium faster
  • Supports deep sleep quality and sleep onset — well documented
  • Involved in 300+ enzymatic reactions including muscle contraction and protein synthesis
  • Helps regulate cortisol and the nervous system response to training stress
  • Reduces muscle cramps and supports recovery between sessions
This is one of the highest-value, lowest-cost interventions for anyone training regularly. Most people notice better sleep within 1-2 weeks.
Omega-3 (EPA + DHA)
2-3g combined EPA+DHA daily · with a fat-containing meal
▶
Protocol
  • Target 2-3g combined EPA + DHA per day (check the label — serving size and actual EPA/DHA content often differ)
  • Take with a meal that contains fat — absorption is significantly better
  • Morning or evening both fine; splitting the dose can reduce fishy aftertaste
  • Refrigerate after opening to slow oxidation
What it actually does
  • Reduces systemic inflammation — important for recovery, joint health, and long-term cardiovascular function
  • Supports muscle protein synthesis in conjunction with adequate protein intake
  • Strong evidence for reducing exercise-induced muscle soreness over time
  • DHA specifically supports brain function, mood regulation, and cognitive sharpness
  • Particularly relevant if fatty fish intake is inconsistent — which it often is on a prep-based system
Look for a product that lists actual EPA and DHA milligrams on the label, not just "fish oil Xg." A typical 1g fish oil capsule may only contain 300mg of actual EPA+DHA.
Vitamin D3 + K2
2,000-5,000 IU D3 daily · with a fat-containing meal
▶
Protocol
  • 2,000-5,000 IU of D3 daily — higher end if you have limited sun exposure
  • Always pair with K2 (100-200mcg MK-7 form) — K2 directs calcium to bones, not arteries
  • Take with a fat-containing meal — D3 is fat-soluble and absorption drops significantly without it
  • Morning is preferable — high D3 taken at night may interfere with melatonin in some people
What it actually does
  • Over 70% of people in Northern latitudes are deficient — especially October through March
  • Critical for testosterone production, immune function, and bone density
  • Deficiency is directly linked to lower strength gains, poor mood, and impaired recovery
  • Supports calcium absorption and cardiovascular health when paired with K2
Get bloodwork to check your 25(OH)D level. Optimal range for performance is generally 40-60 ng/mL. Most people supplementing 2,000 IU/day are still low.
Whey Protein
Convenience tool · not a meal replacement
▶
Protocol
  • Use to hit protein targets on days when food intake falls short — not as the primary strategy
  • Best timing: within 2 hours post-workout if you haven't eaten a full meal
  • Whey isolate absorbs slightly faster than concentrate — useful post-workout; either works fine otherwise
  • Mix with Fairlife for a higher protein shake, or blend into smoothies
What it actually does
  • High leucine content makes it particularly effective at triggering muscle protein synthesis
  • Fast-digesting — peaks in blood amino acids faster than casein or plant proteins
  • Convenient when you need 30-40g of protein without cooking
  • Does not replace the satiety or micronutrients of whole food protein sources
Real food first, whey to fill gaps. A day where you eat 4 solid protein meals and skip the shake is better than a day of shakes and minimal food.
Psyllium Husk
Optional · 5-10g daily · with plenty of water
▶
Protocol
  • 5-10g per day — start low and work up over 2 weeks to avoid gas and bloating
  • Always take with a full glass of water (at least 8 oz) — it expands significantly
  • Can be mixed into smoothies, yogurt, or oatmeal — texture is neutral when blended
  • Use only if digestion is still problematic despite consistently eating beans, potatoes, fruits, and vegetables
What it actually does
  • Soluble fiber that forms a gel in the gut — improves stool consistency in both directions
  • Slows glucose absorption — supports stable blood sugar after high-carb meals
  • Mild cholesterol-lowering effect with consistent use
  • Feeds beneficial gut bacteria
This should be a last resort. If beans, fruit, potatoes, and frozen veg are in the daily rotation and digestion is still off, add this. Otherwise, fix the food first.

System Rules
◆Don't rely on dairy for all protein. Yogurt, cheese, and Fairlife are support pieces — not the whole plan.
◆Beans and frozen veg are not optional. Digestion fix and fullness strategy. At least one bean-based meal daily.
◆Have at least one truly easy meal every day. Protein cereal, yogurt bowl, broth soup, or burrito all count.
◆Never let the freezer go empty. Frozen veg, shrimp, rice, and ground beef keep the system alive on chaotic days.
◆Season boldly. Lean food tastes flat when under-seasoned. Acid, salt, black pepper, and garlic — all of them.
◆Keep it premium enough to want. That's what the steak frites, peppercorn sauce, real cheese, and good potatoes are doing.
◆Freeze components and flavor boosters, not just full meals. Garlic confit, caramelized onions, bean portions, broth cubes.
◆Aim for 25-40g fiber daily. Beans, potato skin, frozen veg, berries, oats, chia seeds.
